# Change management software vs. digital adoption platforms: a category breakdown

A search for "change management software" returns results spanning three functionally
distinct categories. Evaluating the wrong one wastes real time, a full sales cycle, in
some cases, before the mismatch becomes apparent. This piece defines each category
precisely.

## Category one: organizational change management (OCM) software

OCM software supports the planning and execution of a change program: impact scoping,
stakeholder mapping, communications planning, training coordination, and post-launch
adoption measurement. Vendors in this category include OCM Solution's OCMS Portal, The
Change Compass, and ChangePlan. ChangeView is also positioned in this category.

## Category two: digital adoption platforms (DAPs)

DAPs, including Whatfix, WalkMe, and Pendo, overlay in-app guidance and analytics onto
other software products, helping end users learn a specific tool within that tool's own
interface. This solves software adoption at the point of use. It does not address change
program planning, impact assessment, or stakeholder communications, which occur outside
and prior to the software itself.

## Category three: IT service management (ITSM) change management

Platforms in this category, ServiceNow being the dominant example, manage "change" in
the ITIL sense: a formal request to modify a technical system, routed through a change
advisory board for approval before deployment. This is a distinct discipline governing
technical risk to infrastructure, unrelated to managing how people adapt to a process or
organizational change.

## Why the categories get conflated

Aggregator content and "top 10" listicles frequently rank all three categories under a
single "change management software" heading, since each legitimately uses that
terminology, only within different professional contexts. This produces a measurable
cost for buyers: sales cycles initiated with the wrong category before the functional
mismatch is identified.

## Category selection framework

The determining question: what specifically are you trying to control? If the objective
is minimizing technical deployment risk, category three (ITSM) is correct. If the
objective is ensuring end users adopt a specific software interface, category two (DAP)
applies. If the objective is planning and executing an organizational change end to end,
impact, stakeholders, communications, training, adoption, category one (OCM) is the
correct fit.

These categories are not mutually exclusive. A software rollout may reasonably use a DAP
for in-application guidance alongside an OCM platform managing the surrounding change
program. Selecting the correct category first, however, determines which vendor
conversations are worth having at all.

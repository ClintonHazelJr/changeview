# Looking for a Change Compass alternative? Here's what I'd actually check first

The Change Compass is a solid platform, I won't pretend otherwise. It's built for
visualizing change saturation across a large organization running dozens of overlapping
initiatives at once. It's also priced like it: according to pricing published on their
own site, plans start at US$795 per user, per month, for SMB tiers. If you're a solo
consultant or a small team, that's not a small-team price. That's an enterprise number
someone forgot to adjust.

## What it's genuinely good at

If you're running portfolio-level change across a tier-1 bank or a global enterprise,
seeing where saturation is building up before it burns people out is a real problem worth
paying for. That's exactly the buyer Change Compass is built for, and it does that job
well.

## Why it's probably not your problem

Here's the thing though, most of us aren't running forty initiatives across a portfolio.
We're running two or three client engagements, or covering change for a handful of
departments as the one internal hire who does this. If that's you, saturation analytics
aren't what's slowing you down. What's slowing you down is not having a real place to
scope impact, plan comms, and track whether the training actually stuck.

## What I'd actually look for instead

- **Pricing that reflects a small team**, not an enterprise per-seat rate you're paying alone
- **Room for multiple clients or departments**, since most of us aren't running just one thing
- **The same core workflow**, impact, stakeholders, comms, adoption, without paying for a portfolio-saturation layer you won't touch until you're actually managing a portfolio

This is the exact gap I built ChangeView to sit in, same fundamental workflow, priced for
someone actually doing the work, not a transformation office managing forty initiatives
at once.

If what you need today is "a real system instead of a spreadsheet for the three
engagements on my plate right now," that's a smaller, more immediate problem than
saturation analytics. It shouldn't cost an enterprise budget to solve it.

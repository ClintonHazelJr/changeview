# ServiceNow change management vs. what I actually do: same words, completely different jobs

I can't count how many times someone's found my work by searching "change management
software," clicked into ServiceNow, and messaged me confused about why it looks nothing
like what they need. It's not a broken search. It's two professions that happen to share
a name and nothing else.

## What ServiceNow actually means by "change"

In ServiceNow's world, and IT service management generally, a "change" is a technical
request, a server update, a config change, a new deployment, that gets routed through a
change advisory board for approval before it touches production. The entire point is
protecting infrastructure from risk. It's a real discipline. It's just not remotely what
I do.

## What I actually mean by "change"

My version is about people: who's affected, how badly, what they need to know, what
training gets them ready, and whether the thing actually stuck once it launched. No
advisory board approving a server patch. Just stakeholder maps, comms plans, and adoption
tracking, aimed at getting a hundred finance employees comfortable with a process that
just changed under them.

## Why this keeps happening

Both fields grew out of the same instinct, control the risk that comes with change, one
for systems, one for people. So we ended up sharing vocabulary, risk, approval, rollout,
without sharing a single actual deliverable. Sometimes you genuinely need both on the
same project: ServiceNow keeping the technical deployment safe, and someone like me
making sure the people affected by it are communicated with, trained, and actually ready.
Neither one does the other's job.

## How to tell which one you actually need

Ask yourself what you're actually afraid will go wrong. Worried about a system outage or
a botched deployment? You want ITSM change management, ServiceNow or similar. Worried
about people quietly refusing to use the new process, or a training gap tanking adoption
three months in? That's organizational change management, and it's a different search
entirely.

If you've been landing on IT tools while looking for help with the people side, that's
why, and it's worth searching "organizational change management software" instead, a more
precise phrase for a genuinely different job.
